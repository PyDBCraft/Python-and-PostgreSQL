  <h2><a href="#takeaways-from-part-1">Takeaways from Part 1</a></h2>

  In addition to building a proof-of-concept, working through this tutorial should have taught you something about learning how to learn. Here are some notes.

### Notes about developing a project
* Break a project down into small steps
* Ensure each step can be easily tested
* Mock up complex features in replaceable modules
* Document the process, both externally and through internal comments, so that you can explain it to others (including your future self)
* Look for ways to reduce the repitition of code
* Keep sensitive data local and hidden
* Note security issues that will need to be addressed later

### Notes about following a tutorial
* Tutorials are written to make it simple for you to make progress. But learning comes from making mistakes, and understanding where your assumptions are wrong.

* No tutorial is perfect. A tutorial may pass quickly over certain important steps, fail to explain certain details well, or even include factual errors. It's good to question what you read, and ask yourself if there is a better way to achieve the same results.

* After you have successfully built a project from a tutorial, you can build it a second time, from memory. Each time you find yourself wondering what the next step should be, or what the best code for that step should be, you are discovering where your understanding is weak.

* If you understand a process well, you should be able to teach it to others. Teaching others is a good way to check whether you do indeed understand all the processes.
  
### Notes about collaboration
This project was designed to give you something to talk about with others. The more people know about your goals and your progress, the more likely you will be to find someone who can help you to develop your career.
